---
title: Plans and limits
description: What your plan limits, and what happens when you reach a limit.
---

Your license comes with a plan. The Engine applies the plan's limits to all
traffic it receives.

| Limit | What counts |
| --- | --- |
| Input bandwidth | Bytes sent to the Engine in a calendar month (UTC) |
| Output bandwidth | Bytes the Engine sends back in a calendar month (UTC) |
| Requests per second | Incoming HTTP requests and WebSocket messages |
| Entity profiles | How many [entity profiles](/core-concepts/entity-profiles) you can have |

Plans don't charge per thread or per token, and don't cap how many threads you
create. How much history you can keep depends on your own PostgreSQL storage.

## When you reach a limit

Requests over the limit get `429 Too Many Requests`. Short bursts above your
requests-per-second limit are rejected until traffic drops. Once a monthly
bandwidth allowance is used up, requests are rejected until the next month
starts or your plan changes.

People signed in to the dashboard aren't affected by the requests-per-second
limit, so you can still investigate while integrations are being throttled.
Dashboard traffic does count toward bandwidth. Sessions opened with
**Sign in with API key** are limited like the key itself.

Plan changes reach a running Engine within a few minutes. You don't need to
restart it.

## If the Engine can't reach Threadify

A running Engine keeps working with its last known plan if it briefly can't
reach Threadify's licensing service. It needs to verify the license when it
starts, so a restart during an outage will fail until the connection is back.
